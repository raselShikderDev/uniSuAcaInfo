Q1 (Chapter 1). How are information systems transforming business, and why are they essential for running and managing a business today?

Exam-Ready Answer

Information systems (IS) have become essential to modern business because they support nearly every function of an organization, from communication to decision making. Information Technology (IT) refers to the hardware and software needed to achieve business objectives, including computers, storage devices, mobile devices, operating systems, applications, databases, networks, the Internet, intranets, and cloud computing. IT supports communication, automation, decision making, and business processes across the firm.

An Information System goes further than IT alone. It is a set of interrelated components that collect, process, store, and distribute information to support decision making, coordination, control, analysis, and visualization. IS combines IT with people and business processes, meaning technology alone does not create value; it must be integrated with how people work and how the organization is structured.

MIS (Management Information Systems) specifically is a computer-based system that collects, processes, stores, and distributes information so managers receive the right information, at the right time, in the right form, to support their decisions.

IS transform business by helping firms achieve six strategic business objectives: operational excellence (efficiency and productivity), new products, services, and business models, customer and supplier intimacy, improved decision making, competitive advantage, and survival (meeting basic technological and legal requirements to keep operating).

Because business today depends on accurate and timely information for coordination, control, and strategy, and because competitors use IS to gain advantage, a firm without effective information systems risks inefficiency, poor decisions, and even failure to survive in its industry. This is why IS is no longer optional; it is a core requirement of running and managing a business.

Key Points to Memorize

  1. IT = hardware/software; IS = IT + people + business processes.
  2. MIS = computer-based system delivering the right info, right time, right form.
  3. 6 strategic business objectives (memorize the list).
  4. "Survival" objective = meeting basic legal/technological requirements.

Simple Explanation

Think of IT as the tools (computers, apps, networks). An information system is those tools PLUS the people using them PLUS the way work gets done. Businesses need this because good, fast information helps managers make better decisions, beat competitors, and just keep the business legally and technically running.

10-Minute Exam Structure

  1. (1 min) Define IT briefly.
  2. (2 min) Define Information System and MIS, show the difference from IT.
  3. (4 min) List and briefly explain the 6 strategic business objectives.
  4. (2 min) Explain why this makes IS essential (competition, coordination, survival).
  5. (1 min) Short concluding sentence.

Q2 (Chapter 1). What is an information system? How does it work? What are its management, organization, and technology components?

Exam-Ready Answer

An Information System (IS) is a set of interrelated components that collect, process, store, and distribute information to support decision making, coordination, control, analysis, and visualization within an organization. It combines information technology with people and business processes; technology alone is not an information system.

An IS works through four basic functions. Input is the collection of raw data from within the organization or its environment. Processing converts this raw input into a more meaningful form by classifying, arranging, and calculating the data. Output transfers the processed information to the people who will use it, producing reports, summaries, charts, or documents. Feedback is output that is returned to appropriate members of the organization to help evaluate or correct the input stage, forming a continuous cycle.

Beyond the technical process, an IS has three essential dimensions. The organization dimension includes the people, organizational structure, business processes, politics, and culture that shape how the system is used. The management dimension involves decision making, problem solving, strategy, resource allocation, leadership, and coordination; managers decide how the system should be used to meet business goals. The technology dimension consists of the hardware, software, data management technology, and networking/telecommunications that make the system function.

These three dimensions must work together. A system may have excellent technology, but if the organizational processes or management decisions around it are poor, the system will fail to deliver value. This is why IS is described as a sociotechnical system: it depends equally on technology and on the social/organizational context in which it operates.

Key Points to Memorize

  1. 4 IS functions in order: Input → Processing → Output → Feedback.
  2. 3 dimensions: Organization, Management, Technology.
  3. IS = sociotechnical (technology + social/organizational factors).

Simple Explanation

An information system is like a machine that takes in raw data (input), turns it into something useful (processing), gives it to people who need it (output), and then checks itself using that output (feedback). But the "machine" only works well if the right people, structure, and management decisions surround it, that's the organization and management side, not just the tech.

10-Minute Exam Structure

  1. (1.5 min) Define information system.
  2. (3 min) Explain how it works: Input, Processing, Output, Feedback (one sentence each).
  3. (4 min) Explain the 3 dimensions with one example detail for each.
  4. (1.5 min) Conclude: sociotechnical, all 3 dimensions must align.

Q3 (Chapter 1). What academic disciplines are used to study information systems, and how does each contribute to an understanding of information systems?

Exam-Ready Answer

Because information systems are sociotechnical (combining technology with social and organizational factors), studying them requires both a technical approach and a behavioral approach. IS is therefore described as multidisciplinary.

The technical approach draws on three disciplines. Computer Science contributes theories of computability, computing methods, and efficient data storage and access, forming the technical foundation of systems. Management Science contributes the development of models for management decision making and business practice, helping managers apply systematic approaches to problems. Operations Research focuses on mathematical techniques for optimizing selected business activities, such as scheduling or resource allocation, to improve efficiency.

The behavioral approach also draws on three disciplines. Sociology studies how organizations and groups shape, and are shaped by, information systems, for example how systems affect organizational structure or group behavior. Psychology examines how individual people perceive and process information and how they make use of it, which helps explain user behavior and system adoption. Economics studies digital goods and digital markets, and how information systems affect firms' costs and structures of control within the organization.

No single discipline fully explains information systems on its own. The technical disciplines explain how systems are built and optimized, while the behavioral disciplines explain how people and organizations actually use, resist, or adapt to those systems. Combining both approaches gives a complete picture, which is why the field of IS is considered multidisciplinary and sociotechnical rather than purely a branch of computer science.

Key Points to Memorize

  1. Technical approach: Computer Science, Management Science, Operations Research.
  2. Behavioral approach: Sociology, Psychology, Economics.
  3. IS = multidisciplinary and sociotechnical.

Simple Explanation

Some subjects explain the "tech" side (how to build and optimize computer systems): computer science, management science, operations research. Other subjects explain the "people" side (how humans and organizations react to and use systems): sociology, psychology, economics. You need both to fully understand information systems.

10-Minute Exam Structure

  1. (1 min) State IS is multidisciplinary/sociotechnical.
  2. (4 min) Technical approach: name and explain all 3 disciplines.
  3. (4 min) Behavioral approach: name and explain all 3 disciplines.
  4. (1 min) Conclude why both are needed.

Q4 (Chapter 2). What are business processes? How are they related to information systems?

Exam-Ready Answer

A business process is the manner in which work is organized, coordinated, and focused to produce a valuable product or service. It is a collection of activities that are supported by flows of material, information, and knowledge among the participants involved. Business processes describe the specific, repeatable ways an organization gets work done, such as fulfilling an order or hiring an employee.

Information systems are closely tied to business processes because IS can improve how these processes are carried out. Information systems make work faster, easier, and more efficient by automating manual tasks that were previously done by hand. They allow information to be shared quickly among people and departments who need it, and they allow multiple activities to be carried out simultaneously rather than one after another, which reduces delays.

Information systems also reduce delays in decision making by giving decision makers faster access to relevant information. Beyond simply speeding up existing processes, IS can enable the redesign of business processes altogether, and can even support entirely new business models that would not have been possible without the technology, for example fully digital services.

In this way, the relationship works in both directions: business processes shape what an information system needs to do (its requirements come from the process), while information systems shape how a business process can be performed (technology enables new, faster, or redesigned processes). Because of this close relationship, when organizations implement new information systems, they often must also redesign their business processes to fully realize the benefits, rather than simply automating an inefficient process as it already exists.

Key Points to Memorize

  1. Business process = how work is organized/coordinated/focused to create value.
  2. Supported by flows of material, information, knowledge.
  3. IS impact: faster, easier, more efficient; automation; information sharing; simultaneous activities; reduced decision delays; process redesign; new business models.

Simple Explanation

A business process is basically "how the work gets done" step by step, like how an order gets from customer to delivery. Information systems help these processes run faster and smarter, by automating steps, sharing info instantly, letting things happen at the same time instead of one-by-one, and sometimes letting a company invent a totally new way of doing business.

10-Minute Exam Structure

  1. (2 min) Define business process.
  2. (1 min) Mention it is supported by flows of material/information/knowledge.
  3. (5 min) Explain how IS improve business processes (list all effects: automation, sharing, simultaneity, reduced delay, redesign, new models).
  4. (2 min) Conclude on the two-way relationship.

Q5 (Chapter 2). How do systems serve the different management groups in a business, and how do systems that link the enterprise improve organizational performance?

Exam-Ready Answer

Businesses have three management levels, and different types of information systems are designed to serve each level. Operational management handles day-to-day activities and routine decisions, and is served by the Transaction Processing System (TPS), which performs and records daily routine transactions such as sales orders, reservations, payroll, employee records, and shipping. For a TPS, information must be current, accurate, and available at all times.

Middle management is responsible for monitoring, control, and short-to-medium-term decisions, and is served by the Management Information System (MIS), which provides reports on current organizational performance using data drawn from the TPS. MIS usually answers predefined, routine questions rather than open-ended ones.

Middle and other managers facing non-routine problems are also served by the Decision-Support System (DSS), which supports unique and rapidly changing problems where procedures are not fully predefined. A DSS uses internal data from TPS and MIS, and may also use external information, to support sophisticated analysis and modeling.

Senior management makes strategic decisions and long-term plans, and is served by the Executive Support System (ESS), which supports nonroutine strategic decisions requiring judgment, evaluation, and insight. ESS uses summarized internal and external information, often presented through dashboards and key performance indicators.

While TPS, MIS, DSS, and ESS serve individual management levels, enterprise applications span functional areas and management levels to integrate business processes across the whole organization, improving overall organizational performance. The four major enterprise applications are: ERP/Enterprise Systems, integrating manufacturing, finance/accounting, sales/marketing, and HR; SCM, managing supplier, purchasing, distributor, and logistics relationships and information flows; CRM, managing customer-related sales, marketing, and service processes; and KMS, which captures, manages, shares, and applies organizational knowledge and expertise. By linking data and processes across departments instead of keeping them isolated, these systems allow information to flow across the enterprise, improving coordination, efficiency, and decision making at every level.

Key Points to Memorize

  1. Operational → TPS; Middle → MIS & DSS; Senior → ESS.
  2. TPS = current, accurate, available data on routine transactions.
  3. DSS = non-routine, rapidly changing problems, internal + external data.
  4. ESS = strategic, judgment-based, dashboards/KPIs.
  5. 4 Enterprise apps: ERP, SCM, CRM, KMS.

Simple Explanation

Different levels of managers need different types of systems. Front-line/day-to-day staff use TPS (recording sales, payroll etc). Middle managers use MIS (routine performance reports) and DSS (help with tricky one-off problems). Top executives use ESS (big-picture dashboards for strategy). On top of these, enterprise systems (ERP, SCM, CRM, KMS) connect the whole company together so departments aren't working in isolated silos.

10-Minute Exam Structure

  1. (1 min) Intro: 3 management levels need 3 (or 4) system types.
  2. (5 min) Go level by level: Operational–TPS, Middle–MIS/DSS, Senior–ESS. One definition + one distinguishing feature each.
  3. (3 min) Enterprise applications: define, then name and briefly explain ERP, SCM, CRM, KMS.
  4. (1 min) Conclude: enterprise apps link the whole firm, improving performance.

Q6 (Chapter 2). What is the role of the information systems function in a business?

Exam-Ready Answer

The information systems function is the department responsible for managing IT services and infrastructure within a business, including hardware, software, data storage, and networks. This function ensures that the technology backbone of the organization operates reliably and supports the firm's business processes and management needs.

The IS function is carried out by several types of specialists. Programmers are technical specialists who write the software instructions for computers. Systems Analysts act as a link between the information systems department and the rest of the organization, translating business problems and requirements into information requirements and systems. IS Managers lead teams of programmers and analysts, and manage projects, data centers, telecommunications, and other technical operations.

At a senior level, several executive roles oversee IS strategy and governance. The CIO (Chief Information Officer) oversees the organization's use of information technology and works to align technology decisions with overall business strategy. The CSO/CISO (Chief Security Officer / Chief Information Security Officer) is responsible for information systems security. The CPO (Chief Privacy Officer) ensures compliance with data privacy laws and practices. The CKO (Chief Knowledge Officer) manages the organization's knowledge management programs. The CDO (Chief Data Officer) is responsible for enterprise-wide data governance and use.

Overseeing all of this is IT governance: the strategy and policies that guide IT use, including decision rights and accountability, to make sure that IT investments and activities actually support the organization's broader strategy and objectives. In short, the IS function does not just "keep the computers running"; it is responsible for aligning technology, data, security, and knowledge management with what the business is trying to achieve.

Key Points to Memorize

  1. IS department manages IT services/infrastructure: hardware, software, data storage, networks.
  2. Specialist roles: Programmers, Systems Analysts, IS Managers.
  3. Executive roles: CIO, CSO/CISO, CPO, CKO, CDO (know what each oversees).
  4. IT Governance = strategy/policies, decision rights, accountability, alignment with business strategy.

Simple Explanation

The IS function is basically the "tech department" of the company. Programmers write code, systems analysts translate business needs into tech requirements, and IS managers run the technical teams. At the top, executives like the CIO make sure all this technology actually helps the business strategy, while other chiefs (CSO, CPO, CKO, CDO) look after security, privacy, knowledge, and data specifically. IT governance is the rulebook that keeps all these decisions accountable and aligned with the business.

10-Minute Exam Structure

  1. (1.5 min) Define the IS function.
  2. (3 min) Explain the 3 specialist roles.
  3. (4 min) Explain the 5 executive roles (CIO, CSO/CISO, CPO, CKO, CDO).
  4. (1.5 min) Define IT governance and conclude.
