Chapter 1 ### Management Information Systems A Management Information System (\*\*MIS\*\*) is a computer-based system that collects, processes, stores, and distributes information to support communication, automation, business processes, decision making, and coordination across an organization. It gives managers at every level, strategic, tactical, and operational, the right information, in the right form, at the right time, to run and control the business effectively. ## Collection of Data: MIS collects data from different business activities such as sales, employees, customers, and transactions. ## Processing of Data: The collected data is classified, arranged, calculated, and processed to make it meaningful. ## Storage of Information: MIS stores processed information so that it can be accessed and used when required. ## Providing Useful Information: MIS provides information in the form of reports, summaries, charts, and other useful outputs. ## Supporting Management: It provides managers with the right information at the right time to support decision making, coordination, and control. ### Strategic Objectives Information systems are essential because modern businesses depend on accurate and timely information to operate effectively. They help organizations achieve several important strategic objectives: Operational excellence: Improving efficiency and productivity to raise profitability. New products, services, and business models: Use technology to create new ways of doing business. Customer and supplier intimacy: Understand and serve customers and suppliers better. Improved decision making: Provide accurate and timely information instead of relying on guesswork. Competitive advantage: Help firms perform better than competitors. Survival: Help businesses meet technological and legal requirements necessary to continue operating. Therefore, information systems are not simply computer systems. They are important business tools that help organizations operate, manage resources, make decisions, and achieve strategic objectives. Functions of information system An Information System (IS) is a set of interrelated components that collect, process, store, and distribute information to support communication, automation, business processes, decision making, and coordination across an organization. An IS combines technology with people and business processes. An information system works through three basic functions, plus a fourth one that keeps it accurate: Input: Collects raw data, such as customer orders, sales transactions, or employee attendance. Processing: Classifies, arranges, and calculates the data to transform it into useful information. Output: Produces useful results such as reports, summaries, charts, or documents. Feedback: Sends output back to the system to check and correct input when necessary.



Dimension Beyond the technical process, the information system has three major dimensions. Understanding these three dimensions provides information systems literacy, which blends behavioral and technical perspectives rather than focusing solely on computer hardware. ## Organization: Includes people, organizational structure, business processes, politics, and culture. It determines how work and responsibilities are distributed, organized and to be performed. ## Management: Includes decision making, problem solving, resource allocation, leadership, and strategy. Managers use information systems to address business challenges and solve them using tools. ## Technology: Includes computer hardware, software, data management technology, and networking and telecommunications technology. It acts as the physical and digital backbone. Thus, an effective information system requires the proper combination of organization, management, and technology.

\### Business Process



A business process is a structured, repeatable sequence of coordinated tasks and activities designed to achieve a specific organizational goal or produce a consistent outcome. ## Collection of Activities: A business process consists of several activities performed to achieve a particular business result. ## Coordination of Work: Different employees or departments coordinate their activities to complete the process. ## Flow of Information: Information moves between employees, departments, customers, and business partners. ## Cross-functional Nature: Some business processes involve more than one department. For example, fulfilling a customer order may require coordination among sales, accounting, manufacturing, delivery partners, and the customer. Information systems allow information to flow rapidly between these participants. Business Process = How work gets done How IT Improves Business Process Information technology improves business processes by making work faster, easier, more efficient, and better coordinated. It can automate existing activities and also create new ways of doing business. Automation: IT can automate manual activities such as checking customer  credit, generating invoices, and preparing shipping orders. Faster Information Flow: Information can be shared quickly between employees and departments. Simultaneous Activities: IT can allow activities that previously happened one after another to occur simultaneously. Reduced Decision Delays: Managers can receive information quickly and make decisions faster. Creation of New Business Models: Technology can create new activities and business models, such as online shopping, e-books, and music streaming. Therefore, information systems improve business processes by making them faster, more efficient, coordinated, and responsive. Information System = Helps information flow through that work ## How do systems serve the different management groups in a business, and how do systems that link the enterprise improve organizational performance? Systems for Different Groups Organizations have different management levels, so they use different information systems. ## Operational Management Operational managers handle day-to-day activities and routine decisions. They mainly use Transaction Processing Systems (\*\*TPS\*\*). \*\*TPS\*\* records routine transactions such as sales, payroll, shipping, reservations, and employee records. Its information must be current, accurate, and easily available. ## Middle Management Middle managers are responsible for monitoring, control, and short-to-medium-term decisions. They use Management Information Systems (\*\*MIS\*\*). \*\*MIS\*\* summarizes data from \*\*TPS\*\* and produces routine reports that help managers monitor current performance and control the business. ## Senior Management Senior managers focus on strategic decisions and long-term planning. They use Executive Support Systems (\*\*ESS\*\*). \*\*ESS\*\* provides summarized internal and external information through dashboards and other easy-to-use interfaces. It helps senior managers make nonroutine decisions requiring judgment and insight. Very important exam structure: Operational → \*\*TPS\*\* → Routine transactions Middle      → \*\*MIS\*\* → Monitoring and control Senior      → \*\*ESS\*\* → Strategy and long-term decisions



\### Enterprise Application



Enterprise applications connect different functional areas and management levels. They improve organizational performance by integrating business processes and allowing information to flow across departments. The four major enterprise applications are: \*\*ERP\*\*: Integrates major internal business functions. \*\*SCM\*\*: Manages relationships and information flow with suppliers and logistics partners. \*\*CRM\*\*: Manages customer sales, marketing, and service relationships. \*\*KMS\*\*: Captures and distributes organizational knowledge and expertise. Therefore, management systems provide appropriate information at different organizational levels, while enterprise applications integrate departments and processes, improving coordination, flexibility, productivity, customer service, and resource management. Very important exam structure:



Enterprise: \*\*ERP\*\* → Internal functions \*\*SCM\*\* → Suppliers \*\*CRM\*\* → Customers \*\*KMS\*\* → Knowledge



\## What is the role of the information systems function in a business?



The information systems function is responsible for managing and maintaining the technology and information systems that support business operations. In most organizations, the Information Systems Department is responsible for IT services, including: Hardware Software Data storage Networks IT infrastructure Important IS specialists include: Programmers: Write software instructions (codes) for computers. Systems Analysts: Connect the IS department with the rest of the organization and translate business requirements into information systems. Information Systems Managers: Manage programmers, analysts, projects, facilities, telecommunications, databases, and computer operations. Chief Information Office (\*\*CIO\*\*): Oversees the organization's use of IT and aligns technology with business strategy. Other senior roles may include \*\*CSO\*\*/\*\*CISO\*\*, \*\*CPO\*\*, \*\*CKO\*\*, and \*\*CDO\*\*, responsible respectively for security, privacy, knowledge management, and data governance. The IS function has also evolved from mainly performing technical programming tasks to acting as a strategic business partner and change agent. IS professionals can propose new strategies, information-based products, services, and business improvements. Finally, IT governance establishes the strategy, policies, decision rights, and accountability for using IT so that technology supports organizational objectives. Remember: IS Function = Manage Technology + Support Business + Align IT with Strategy Chief Information Officer = \*\*CIO\*\* Chief Security Officer = \*\*CSO\*\* Chief Information Security Officer = \*\*CISO\*\* Chief Privacy Officer = \*\*CPO\*\* Chief Knowledge Officer = \*\*CKO\*\* Chief Data Officer = \*\*CDO\*\*
